"How much does an ebook cost" has no single answer, because an ebook is not one purchase: it is four separate line items that most guides quote in isolation. Ghostwriting has its own market rate. Typesetting software has its own. Cover design and formatting each have theirs. Add them up the traditional way and the number that appears has almost nothing to do with the $10–20 "AI ebook" ads you've probably seen.

This is what each piece actually costs right now, priced from the sellers themselves, then what happens when one tool does all four at once.

## Ghostwriting: the line item that decides everything

Writing is where the traditional route spends most of its budget, and the spread is enormous because "an ebook" covers wildly different jobs.

For a **short ebook used as a lead magnet or course supplement** (roughly 10,000–25,000 words), [Reedsy's marketplace data](https://reedsy.com/blog/cost-to-hire-a-ghostwriter/) puts the price at **$1,500–$8,000**. For a **full-length nonfiction book**, Reedsy ghostwriters charge an average of **$0.60 per word** in the general nonfiction category (business, health, self-help) — an 80,000-word manuscript averages **$32,800**, with quotes commonly landing in the **$18,000–$50,000** range and top-tier ghostwriters asking $50,000–$100,000 for a full manuscript from scratch. On top of the writer's fee, Reedsy adds a 20% platform commission split between both sides of the deal, so the number you actually pay is higher than the base quote.

None of that includes research. A ghostwriter who isn't a subject-matter expert either bills extra hours to research your topic, or writes from general knowledge, which is exactly how error-riddled "expert" ebooks happen.

## Typesetting: the part every DIY budget forgets

Once you have a manuscript, it is not a book. It's a Word document. Turning it into something with real margins, a clickable table of contents, drop caps and consistent hyphenation needs dedicated software or a freelancer.

The two names self-publishers actually use:

- **Atticus**: **$147**, one-time, cross-platform (Windows, Mac, web), lifetime updates included.
- **Vellum**: **$199.99** for ebook-only export, **$249.99** for the ebook+print bundle ("Vellum Press"), Mac-only.

Both tools are excellent at layout and format **zero words of your book**. You still arrive with a finished manuscript. If you'd rather pay someone to run the software for you, freelance book formatting on Fiverr clusters in the **$45–$195** range for a straightforward job, with specialists charging $300 and up; the marketplace-wide spread runs roughly **$10 to $500+** depending on complexity and how much hand-holding you need.

## Cover design: always a separate invoice

A cover is never bundled into a writing or formatting purchase. It's its own line item, and quality tracks price closely here:

- **Premade/template covers**: **$50–$500**.
- **Custom illustrated or photo-composite covers**: **$500–$2,000**, with the average professional custom cover landing around **$400–$1,200**.
- **Fiverr**, specifically: beginner sellers from $20–$100, established sellers' best work in the **$150–$400** band.
- **99designs**: contests start at **$299**, full range **$279–$999**.

Add a fourth job (proofreading or a final editorial pass) and most guides put that at another $200–$800 for a short manuscript.

## Stacked, the traditional route looks like this

Take the cheapest realistic path for a modest, 15,000-word lead-magnet-length ebook: a $1,500 ghostwriter, $50 Fiverr formatting, a $50 premade cover. That's **$1,600** before you've spent a cent on ads or launched anything, and it assumes you got lucky on quality at every budget-tier step.

Take the credible mid-market path for an actual nonfiction book: a $25,000 ghostwriter, $200 for Vellum, a $600 custom cover, $400 for a proofread. That's **over $26,000**, plus weeks of back-and-forth briefing, drafts and revisions with every vendor separately.

Free "AI ebook generator" tools sit at the opposite, equally misleading end: they hand you an unformatted draft with a watermark, no commercial license, and none of the three other jobs done. You still pay for typesetting and a cover afterward, just with an extra step.

[InkMagnet](/) exists because those four line items are really one job: research the topic, write every chapter, typeset it properly, design a cover that fits the content, and export a finished file. Doing it as one pipeline instead of four separate purchases is what makes **$14.99–$59.99 per book** (by length, one-time, full commercial rights, unlimited edits and recompiles) realistic rather than suspicious: there's no ghostwriter's hourly margin, no formatter's separate invoice, no designer's contest fee stacked on top, because none of those are separate vendors here.

That's also the honest caveat: a $32,800 ghostwritten, professionally typeset, custom-covered book from named human specialists is a different product with different guarantees than an AI-researched and AI-typeset one. For a lead magnet, a course companion, a KDP title or an internal knowledge base, the gap in outcome is small and the gap in price is not. For a flagship book carrying your name at $30 a copy in stores, that calculus can flip.
