Search "vellum alternative" or "atticus alternative" and the price shows up in the first paragraph every time: $147, $199.99, $249.99, because it's the one fact both companies can't soften. What almost never shows up in the same paragraph is that neither tool writes a word of the book you're about to format. You arrive with a finished manuscript or you don't get past the import screen. That's not a knock on either product. Both are the real standard-bearers of independent book typesetting, checked directly on their own pricing pages in August 2026. It just means the price tag buys exactly one stage of a four-stage job, and it isn't the stage most people actually get stuck on.

## What $147 and $199.99–249.99 actually buy

[Atticus](https://www.atticus.io) is $147, one-time, with lifetime updates included, and it runs on Windows, Mac, Linux and Chromebook through the browser, the widest platform reach of any dedicated formatting tool. That single price covers 17+ chapter themes with over a thousand style combinations, a custom theme builder, full-bleed image support and direct export for Amazon KDP and IngramSpark.

[Vellum](https://vellum.pub) works differently: you can write, design and preview a book inside it for free, and only pay when you export final files. That's $199.99 for ebook formats alone, or $249.99 for the "Vellum Press" bundle that adds print-ready PDF. The catch is the platform: Vellum is Mac-only and needs macOS 13 or newer, with no Windows version planned, ever. In exchange you get a typesetting engine that handles widows, orphans, ligatures and optical margin alignment at a level reviewers consistently rate above Atticus's output.

Both numbers land inside the $147–250 range InkMagnet's own [cost breakdown](/blog/cost-to-create-an-ebook/) already cites as the going rate for dedicated formatting software, and neither price includes anything upstream of formatting. No research pass, no drafting, no editorial review — just the tool that turns a finished Word file into a distributable book.

## Where the words are supposed to come from

This is the part the price comparisons skip. Atticus has no AI writing features at all: no autocomplete, no outline help, no chapter drafting. It integrates with ProWritingAid for grammar and style checking, but that's rule-based proofreading applied to text you already typed, not generation. Vellum is the same shape from the other direction. You either write inside its own editor or import a Word document, and either way the 30,000 to 90,000 words that make up a typical nonfiction book have to exist before you ever open the app.

Neither tool is hiding this; it's the deliberate scope of a formatting product. Take a finished manuscript and make it look like it came from a publisher's typesetting department. But "finished manuscript" is doing a lot of quiet work in that sentence. Research, structure, drafting and revision all happen somewhere else, on someone else's clock and someone else's budget, before either license fee ever earns its keep.

## When Atticus or Vellum is the right call

If the manuscript already exists (a novel drafted over the past year, a memoir already finished, a client's raw text you're contracted to format and nothing else), Atticus or Vellum is the right tool, not a workaround. Atticus wins on platform reach and a lower entry price; Vellum wins on typographic polish if you're already inside the Apple ecosystem and want a matching print edition too.
