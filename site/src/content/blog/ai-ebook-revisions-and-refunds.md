The book comes back and something's off. Maybe the tone leans more academic than the casual guide you asked for, maybe a chapter drifts past the brief, maybe you just don't love it yet. Every comparison of AI book tools talks price and page count. Almost none of them say what happens next, and that's the question that actually decides whether a purchase was safe.

## What a human ghostwriter promises, and what it costs to go past it

A professionally drafted ghostwriting contract usually bakes in two to three revision rounds as part of the flat fee, with the scope of a "round" spelled out up front so client and writer agree on what counts as a fix versus a rewrite. Go past that number and the price jumps fast: an extra round commonly adds somewhere between $1,000 and $5,000 depending on the project, or bills out at $100-$300 an hour if the contract runs hourly instead. On Fiverr, where per-project prices are far lower, the ceiling is lower too: sellers typically cap revisions at one to three rounds, unlimited revisions are the exception rather than the rule, and it's on the buyer to confirm the number before paying, because gig descriptions vary writer to writer.

None of that is a knock on ghostwriters. A human rewriting a chapter by hand is real, billable labor, and a cap protects the writer from an open-ended project. It does mean the safety net has a hard edge: round three goes fine, round four costs extra.

## What already happened before you saw the draft

InkMagnet doesn't hand you a first pass and start the clock on revision rounds. [The pipeline](/blog/how-ai-writes-a-book/) plans the whole book first: chapters, sections, a research brief per section, before a sentence gets written, then drafts against that plan so chapters don't repeat each other or drift off it individually. After the draft, a separate AI editing pass checks coverage against the brief, trims what's redundant and fills what's thin, the same reviewed-then-revised step described in the product's own sourcing page. That happens automatically, before the file ever reaches your downloads folder. It's not a service you request or pay to unlock.

It also isn't a substitute for you reading the result. The brief you write is the only description of what you wanted; if it under-specifies tone or audience, the review pass has nothing sharper to check against than what you gave it. A one-line topic produces a broader, more generic book than a brief that names the reader, the angle and three things it should absolutely cover.

## The refund answer, in plain terms

Here's the part most competitors bury: [InkMagnet's terms](/terms) state that because generation starts immediately and produces content personalized to your brief, a purchase becomes non-refundable once generation has started, except where consumer law says otherwise. The one guaranteed refund case is narrower and unconditional: if generation fails for technical reasons and it can't be fixed, the book is refunded. That's the whole policy, not a summary of a longer one.

It's worth seeing that against how the template and subscription tools handle it, because "AI book generator" as a category covers genuinely different products. Sqribble sells a template-filling tool, not personalized writing, and backs it with a 30-day no-questions-asked refund. The trade-off is that a refund also revokes your account access to the templates you bought. Squibler, sold as a subscription, gives EU and UK subscribers a 14-day withdrawal window and everyone else 7 days, with the refund reduced by the value of whatever you generated with the subscription during that window before you cancel. Both policies make sense for what those tools actually sell: a template library or a period of app access, not a finished, personalized manuscript generated once against your specific brief.

That distinction — a reusable tool license versus a one-off personalized output — is exactly the line EU consumer law draws around digital content, and it's why InkMagnet's terms read the way they do rather than copying either model wholesale. A subscription can be pro-rated and walked back mid-period. A book already written against your topic, structure and chosen angle can't be un-written the same way a software seat can be revoked.
