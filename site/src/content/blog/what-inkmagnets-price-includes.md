A $67 "one-time" ebook tool and a $9.99 InkMagnet book look like they're competing on the same line in a comparison table. They aren't, once you look past the first screen. The $67 tool usually charges for a template filler and stops there: a 3D cover option, extra template packs and a "find you clients" add-on show up as separate upsells the moment you land on the thank-you page. The InkMagnet number is the total you pay, not the entry point to a checkout flow with three more offers behind it. Here is what that difference actually looks like, line by line, using current market rates rather than the marketing page's own claims.

## What the sticker price doesn't say

[BookBaby](https://support.bookbaby.com/hc/en-us/articles/205518128-How-much-does-it-cost-to-use-BookBaby-services)'s ebook conversion package starts at **$299**, and that figure covers only the conversion itself. Interior formatting is a separate **$149**. A custom cover runs **$199 to $699** on top of that. An ISBN adds **$79**. Embedded images bill at **$3 each**. Run a finished 200-page manuscript through the full stack, and support's own worked example on their site lands around **$1,176** total, for a book someone had already written before any of those charges started (rates checked September 2026).

[Sqribble](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/sqribble) sells its base package at a discounted **$67**, loaded with 50 templates. Underneath that front-end price sit four separate upsells: Professional, unlocking 150 more templates; Prime, a monthly drip of new template packs; Fantasia 3D, the tool that turns a flat cover into the glossy 3D render most of the marketing screenshots actually show; and a job-finder add-on aimed at people reselling the service. The base $67 does not include that 3D cover tool. It's a purchase of its own.

[Squibler](https://reedsy.com/studio/resources/squibler-review/)'s Plus plan runs **$29.99 a month**, Pro **$89.99 a month**. Both tiers generate an AI cover image, but reviewers are specific about what that means: it's an illustration, not a print-spec book cover formatted for KDP or a printer, and the lower tier doesn't export EPUB at all. Get the draft text and a cover picture out of a subscription like that, and a formatter or a cover designer is still a separate line item standing between you and a file you can actually sell anywhere.

None of this makes those tools dishonest. It's the ordinary software playbook: a low anchor price up front, then modular add-ons priced separately for whatever the anchor didn't cover. It just means the number printed on the pricing page and the number you actually pay for a finished, formatted, cover-ready book are two different numbers, and the gap between them rarely shows up until you're already a customer.

## What InkMagnet's price is the total for

InkMagnet's tiers run from **$9.99** for a 30 to 45 page Compact book up to **$34.99** for a 161 to 200 page Complete edition. That figure is what you pay, full stop, for all of the following:

- **Research**: the engine reads live web sources on the topic before writing a single sentence, not from training-data memory alone.
- **Writing and review**: every chapter is drafted, then checked against the others for consistency, not left as one unedited first pass.
- **Typesetting**: a real book layout with a clickable table of contents, styled headings, drop caps and tables, instead of a Word document exported to PDF.
- **Illustrations**: optional AI artwork generated per chapter when a topic calls for it, matched to the book's language and region.
- **Cover**: a designed, print-spec cover file, not a placeholder template you're expected to customize yourself.
- **PDF and EPUB files**: both formats included, ready to upload to KDP, a website or a customer's inbox.
- **Full commercial rights**: sell it, give it away as a lead magnet, publish it under your own name, effective the moment the file downloads.
- **Unlimited edits and recompiles**: every book you've bought stays editable indefinitely, with no extra charge to regenerate the PDF or EPUB after a change.

There's no second screen asking whether you'd like the cover as an add-on, and no tier where EPUB export sits locked behind a more expensive plan.

## Running the same book through both models

Take a 120-page nonfiction guide, roughly 30,000 words, and price it the itemized way most tools actually bill it. A template-filling tool gets you a formatted shell for around $67, but the writing itself is still yours to produce, so a real edit follows: [copy editing alone runs $0.021 to $0.04 a word](https://www.editorworld.com/article/how-much-does-book-editing-cost) at standard turnaround, which puts a 30,000-word manuscript at roughly **$630 to $1,200** (rates checked September 2026). Add a [custom cover in the realistic $300 to $800 range](https://reedsy.com/blog/book-cover-design-cost/) that most authors end up paying once they see the template options, plus interior formatting comparable to BookBaby's $149 line item, and the itemized total for that same finished book lands somewhere between roughly $1,150 and $2,200, and EPUB conversion hasn't entered the math yet.

InkMagnet's Comprehensive tier, 116 to 160 pages, prices the equivalent scope at **$27.99**: written, reviewed, typeset, covered, and exported as both PDF and EPUB. That is a fraction of a single line item on the itemized version, let alone all of them added together.
