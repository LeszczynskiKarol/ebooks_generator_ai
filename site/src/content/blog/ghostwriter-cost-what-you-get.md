"How much does a ghostwriter cost" is the wrong first question. The number swings from $1,500 to $50,000 depending on who you ask, and every one of those figures is honest for a different job. The question that actually decides your budget is what's inside that number, because a ghostwriting fee buys less than most people assume, and what it doesn't buy is where the real cost hides.

## The three ways ghostwriters price a book

Ghostwriters quote in one of three formats, and each hides a different assumption.

**Hourly**: rates run **$35–$350 an hour**, with the [Editorial Freelancers Association](https://www.the-efa.org) reporting **$70–$188/hour** across ghostwriting project types, and the average ghostwriter hourly pay landing around **$32** in 2026. Marketplace freelancers on Upwork cluster lower, roughly **$20–$45/hour**.

**Per word**: professional nonfiction ghostwriters charge **$0.30–$2.00+ per word**, with experienced specialists asking $0.50–$3.00 and up. Marketplace writers on Fiverr and Upwork undercut that badly, often **$0.03–$0.15 a word**, which is also where the quality complaints concentrate.

**Per project**, the figure most people actually compare: [Reedsy's marketplace data](https://reedsy.com/blog/cost-to-hire-a-ghostwriter/) puts a short lead-magnet-length book (10,000–25,000 words) at **$1,500–$8,000**, and a full nonfiction book (60,000–90,000 words) at **$18,000–$50,000**, with an 80,000-word manuscript averaging **$32,800**. Multiple ghostwriting agencies now advise clients to be skeptical of any quote under $10,000 for a full-length nonfiction book. Below that line, the corners cut are usually research depth and revision rounds, not the writer's basic competence.

The $0.03–$0.15-a-word marketplace tier deserves its own warning. It's also the bracket where writers take on more projects than they can properly research, because the per-word rate only pencils out at high volume. A cheap ghostwriter and a thinly-researched ebook are frequently the same purchase.

## What the fee doesn't include

None of those numbers are the whole job.

**Revisions are capped.** One to three rounds per milestone is standard practice; a ghostwriter offering unlimited revisions is treated as a red flag in the industry, not a selling point, because it usually signals a first draft that wasn't researched properly the first time.

**Rights transfer late.** The standard structure is work-for-hire: you own the manuscript once you've paid in full, not before. Walk away mid-project over a dispute and the draft you've already funded isn't legally yours yet.

**Formatting and cover are separate purchases.** A ghostwriter hands you a Word document. Turning it into a real book needs typesetting software like Atticus ($147) or Vellum ($199.99–$249.99), a freelance formatter ($200–$1,000), and a cover designer on top of that. [Our full breakdown of what each stage costs](/blog/cost-to-create-an-ebook/) prices all three separately.

**It's slow.** Most ghostwriters spend one to two hours per page just drafting, before editing, revising and proofreading start. A short book still needs a 4–6 week interview and outlining phase before a word gets written, and a full-length nonfiction book commonly takes **3 months to a year** start to finish, longer if your feedback rounds stall.

**An NDA is standard**, and worth having, since you're handing a stranger your unpublished ideas before any of the above happens.

## The realistic bill for a book like yours

Take the lead-magnet-sized job most InkMagnet customers are actually solving for: a 15,000–20,000-word nonfiction guide meant to sit behind an email signup or ship with an online course. The writing alone runs $1,500–$8,000 on Reedsy's figures. Add formatting (Atticus at $147, Vellum at $199.99–$249.99, or a freelancer at $200–$1,000) and a cover ($50–$500 for anything a reader would trust). The realistic total for a finished, sellable file lands somewhere between **$1,700 and $9,500**, and that's before any of the three vendors have talked to each other. You're the one relaying feedback between a writer, a formatter and a designer who've never seen each other's work, on three separate timelines you have to coordinate yourself.

Run the numbers on a full 80,000-word nonfiction book instead and the gap only widens: $18,000–$50,000 for the manuscript, plus the same formatting and cover costs on top, plus proofreading most guides price at another $200–$800. A ghostwritten, professionally typeset, custom-covered book from named specialists is a real product with real guarantees. It's just a different budget entirely from a lead magnet or a course companion.
