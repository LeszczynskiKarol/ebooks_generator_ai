A nonfiction guide with eight chapters usually wants eight pieces of art to break up the text: one per chapter, matched to what that chapter actually covers. Outside InkMagnet, that's a separate line item you have to source, brief and pay for, on top of the writing and the cover. Here's what that line item actually costs at current market rates, and why InkMagnet's price doesn't have one.

## What a freelance illustrator charges

Freelance illustration rates run wide depending on experience: junior illustrators around $20-$40 an hour, mid-level $50-$100, senior or specialist illustrators $75-$150 and up. For a single spot illustration, the going rate is roughly **$50 to $500** depending on style and complexity, and picture-book-quality work sits at the higher end of that, **$120 to $500+** per image (rates checked September 2026).

Scale that to a full nonfiction manuscript. A book needing 8 to 10 chapter illustrations at even a modest $150 average lands at **$1,200 to $1,500**, before you've paid anyone to write a word or design a cover. Full illustrated projects, like a 32-page children's book with 15-17 pieces, routinely run **$2,500 to $8,500** in total. None of that includes the back-and-forth of briefing an artist on tone, revising drafts, or matching illustration style to a book's language and target market.

## What a stock or AI-art subscription charges

The cheaper-looking alternative is a subscription instead of a commission, and it still isn't free.

[Shutterstock](https://www.shutterstock.com/)'s entry tier is 10 images a month for $25, billed annually at $300 a year, which works out to $2.50 per image; its Unlimited plan is $29 a month billed annually. [Adobe Stock](https://stock.adobe.com/) prices its 10-image tier at roughly $3.00 per image on annual billing, with monthly plans starting around $29.99 (rates checked September 2026). Either way, you're picking stock photography that wasn't made for your book, not art generated for your specific chapter content.

[Midjourney](https://www.midjourney.com/) bills by GPU time rather than per image: Basic runs $10 a month, Standard $30, Pro $60, Mega $120, with roughly 20% off on annual billing (rates checked September 2026). That buys you a prompting tool, not a finished, placed illustration. You still write the prompts, review the outputs, pick the keepers, and drop each one into your manuscript's layout by hand, chapter by chapter.

Either route means a recurring subscription commitment plus your own time doing the matching and placing work that a book's design should be doing for you.

## Why "matched to language and region" matters

A stock photo library doesn't know what a chapter is about, and a prompting tool doesn't know it's illustrating a book meant for a Polish reader versus a US one. Sourcing that fit yourself means checking every image against the chapter's actual content, then again against tone, market and language, which is exactly the kind of unpaid hour that turns a $150 illustration into a half-day project. InkMagnet's pipeline reads the chapter it's illustrating and generates artwork against that content directly, in the book's own language and region, so the picture in chapter four actually matches what chapter four says rather than a stock keyword search that got close enough.

## What happens if you want changes

Commission a freelancer and a revision round is a new invoice or, at best, a clause in the original brief that caps how many rounds you get for free. A stock subscription doesn't have a revision concept at all: if the photo doesn't fit, you search again and burn another credit. InkMagnet's illustrations live inside the same editable book as everything else, so if a chapter's artwork doesn't land, you regenerate it or swap it in the built-in editor without opening a new contract or paying for a second license.
